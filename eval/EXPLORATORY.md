## E2. X02 two-stage retrieval (session first, then rounds inside the top S sessions), pilot, level H3

Committed before the run. Level: H3 (the registered level: own + whole deduplicated pool minus the content cluster), pilot, 94 questions with gold (abstention excluded), 90 content clusters; the 23 pool-level low-overlap questions (results/pilot/lowoverlap_pool_h3.json, threshold 100) are reported separately. Retriever: exact in-process (BM25 and E5 vectors only; NO date, as-of or id field: distractor dates are synthetic and would leak the label).
- Stage 1 (session ranking over the level's sessions): RRF (rank constant 60) of (a) session-level BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75, the stoplist tokeniser of the exact retriever, term frequencies summed over the session's chunks, document frequency counted over sessions, length = tokens of the session) and (b) cosine between the query vector and the L2-normalised mean of the session's chunk vectors.
- Stage 2: the existing round-level hybrid (RRF 60 over exact BM25 and exact dense, global statistics unchanged) restricted to the top S sessions of stage 1, S in {20, 50} fixed in advance; session recall@10 is read from the resulting round ranking (best round per session), exactly as for the flat arm.
- Baseline: flat RRF session recall@10 at H3 recomputed in the same run (0.4754 expected).
- Pass rule (per S): the paired difference (two-stage minus flat) in session recall@10 is at least +0.10 AND the lower bound of its 95% paired, content-cluster-clustered bootstrap interval is above 0 (`clustered_paired_bootstrap`, 2,000 resamples, seed 16096). The two S values are two pre-declared variants; each is judged on its own and none is declared the winner after the fact.
- Label-blind probe, run FIRST: AUROC of session length (tokens) for separating the gold sessions from the other sessions of the same question's H3 level (mean over questions), against a 1,000-permutation null (gold labels permuted within question, seeded); if the real AUROC lies outside the null's 2.5th to 97.5th percentile, stage-1 features carry a length shortcut: this is REPORTED, and E2 is still run but flagged as "not label-blind" (BM25 with b = 0.75 and mean pooling depend on length).
- Controls that must fail: (C1) stage 1 with session text and vectors shuffled across sessions (a seeded permutation of which session's document each session id carries, stage 2 unchanged): it must NOT pass (its improvement lower bound must be <= 0) and its mean session recall@10 must be <= the flat mean; (C2) planted-gold sanity: stage 1 is replaced by the oracle "top-S plus the question's gold sessions" (S = 50): stage-2 session recall@10 must be at least 0.90 (shows that stage 2 and the metric can find gold when it is present, so gold presence in the top S is the binding constraint).
- Diagnostics: stage-1 recall@S (the ceiling for that S); hit rate (any gold session in the top 10) with a Wilson 95% interval whose n is the number of content clusters (cluster hit rate = mean over its questions; the Wilson formula is applied to the cluster-level mean); results for all 94, the 23 low-overlap and the other 71.
- By-product X01 (no claim about context rot): presence of any gold session, and of all gold sessions, in a packed context at budgets 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000 / 8,000 / 16,000 / 32,000 tokens (strict-prefix `pack_rag`, ceil(chars/4) tokens) at H1, H2, H3 for the flat RRF ranking and for the best-passing two-stage variant, Wilson by content cluster; control for the presence metric: a ranking that puts the gold round first must give presence 1 for every budget that fits that round (planted-gold check; replaces the random-order control, which is zero by construction).
- Timing is not reported (the host is loaded).

## E3. X05 retrieval-confidence calibration (pilot, levels H0, H1, H2, H3)

Committed before the run. Question: do the top retrieval scores predict whether the gold session is among the top 10 sessions of the flat RRF-60 ranking at that level? Retriever: exact in-process; embedder `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` revision 614241f; pilot, 94 questions with gold, 90 content clusters (the 8 pilot abstention questions are used only for the score-distribution report). One analysis per pool level (scores and prevalence change with the pool, so nothing is pooled across levels).
- Target: 1 if any gold session is in the top 10 sessions of the flat RRF ranking at that level, else 0.
- Features (per question, from the exact BM25 and dense round lists at that level, depth 200): dense top-1 cosine; dense margin (top-1 minus top-10 cosine); BM25 margin ratio (top-10 score divided by top-1 score); agreement (overlap, out of 10, between the top-10 sessions of the BM25 and the dense lists). No rank-only RRF score is used (a rank-fused score has almost no information about confidence).
- Model: logistic regression (L2 1.0, standardised features), fit by IRLS in numpy. Evaluation: 5-fold cross-validation GROUPED BY CONTENT CLUSTER (cluster order by sha256("16096:cv:<cluster id>"), folds by position modulo 5); AUROC computed on the pooled out-of-fold predictions (Mann-Whitney, ties averaged).
- Null: 1,000 permutations of the target across questions (seed 16096), the whole cross-validated pipeline repeated for each. PASS at a level iff the real AUROC exceeds the 97.5th percentile of that null. No other pass criterion (the earlier "AUROC >= 0.75 with lower bound 0.70" and the 0.45 to 0.55 band are not used: at n = 94 the null AUROC has a standard deviation of about 0.06).
- Descriptive, not a pass criterion: the out-of-fold precision of the "confident" group = the 30% of questions with the highest predicted probability (share with gold in the top 10, Wilson 95% by content cluster) against the level's base rate; the raw dense top-1 cosine at the 30% cut. Any threshold is valid only for this embedder (revision above), this pool level and this exact pipeline; it is not transferable to another embedder generation or pool size.
- Also reported: mean dense top-1 cosine and BM25 margin of the 8 pilot abstention questions against the answerable ones, per level (descriptive).

- Verdict: no gain at either S; the control that must fail failed to improve as required; the sanity control did not meet its bar; the probe says the features are not label-blind. The idea did not hold on this data with these features. Evidence class: inferred, not proven.

# Round 2 (candidate generation): E4, E5, E6. Same rules as above: pilot only, no model, exact in-process retriever unless stated, thresholds committed before each run.

Common definitions (committed now). Level H3 (own + whole deduplicated pool minus the content cluster), pilot, 94 questions with gold, 90 content clusters; the 23 pool-level low-overlap questions (results/pilot/lowoverlap_pool_h3.json, threshold 100) are reported separately from the 71 others. A candidate list is a ranking of ROUNDS (the unit handed to a reordering arm; the analysts received the top 50 rounds). For a list cut at K rounds: HIT@K = share of questions with at least one round of a gold session among the top K; SESSION-RECALL@K = mean fraction of the question's gold sessions that have at least one round among the top K. Session recall@10 (first 10 distinct sessions of the ranking) stays the headline of the earlier tables. Paired intervals: `clustered_paired_bootstrap`, content clusters, 2,000 resamples, seed 16096. Flat baseline = RRF 60 over exact BM25 and exact dense round lists (depth 1,000 each).

## E4. Candidate ceiling
Report HIT@K and SESSION-RECALL@K for K = 50, 100, 200, 400, 1,000 rounds at H3 for BM25, dense and flat RRF, on all 94 and on the low-overlap 23 (Wilson 95% by content cluster for HIT@K), plus the mean number of distinct sessions inside the top K. The cross-encoder rerank v2 rankings (head 20 reordered, tail unchanged) are reported at K = 20, 50, 100 for the questions that finished; a cross-encoder over a deeper head is E5(d). Descriptive; no pass rule. It states how deep a first stage must go before any reordering arm can help (a reordering arm cannot exceed HIT@K of its candidate list).

## E5. Cheap first-stage variants (H3), each against flat RRF
Variants (all deterministic, no model): (a) session-sum pooling: take the flat top 400 rounds with their RRF scores, score each SESSION by the sum of its rounds' scores, order rounds by (session score, round score) (the baseline is round-level max); (b) rare-term/entity-boosted BM25: each query term's contribution is multiplied by idf / mean idf of the query's terms (so idf squared overall) and by 1.5 for digit tokens and capitalised words not at the start of the question, fused by RRF 60 with the unchanged dense list; (c) deterministic query expansion: pseudo-relevance feedback from the flat top 5 rounds (the 8 highest tf x idf terms not already in the query, weight 0.4, added to BM25) and a Rocchio dense query (q + 0.5 x mean vector of the top 3 dense chunks, renormalised), fused by RRF 60; (d) the cross-encoder (bge-reranker-v2-m3, chunk-max, the registered input rule) over the flat top 200 and top 400 rounds instead of the top 20, run on the 23 low-overlap questions only (cost), reporting seconds per question and pairs scored; K = 400 only if K = 200 shows the minimum gain below.
- Pre-committed threshold for (a) to (c): a variant HELD iff its SESSION-RECALL@50 (rounds) on the low-overlap 23 exceeds flat RRF by at least +0.05 AND the lower bound of the 95% paired interval of that difference is above 0. Results on all 94 are secondary. For (d) the metric is session recall@10 after the rerank: HELD iff the mean gain over flat RRF on the low-overlap 23 is at least +0.05 AND the paired interval's lower bound is above 0.
- Control that must fail to help: the same variant with the query replaced by another question's query (a seeded derangement of the 94 pilot questions, applied to the BM25 terms, the dense vector and the feedback step), compared with FLAT RRF run on the same wrong query: the variant must NOT gain +0.05 over flat on that wrong query (it also must not exceed the real-query result). A variant whose control gains +0.05 or more is not credited.
- If any variant holds on the exact retriever it is re-run on Elasticsearch 9.5 (Basic features only: BM25 and kNN, client-side fusion) with the parity reported; nothing here is an Elasticsearch result.

## E6. Length shortcut
Session length (tokens of its chunks) separates gold sessions from the other sessions of the H3 level (AUROC 0.687 against a 1,000-permutation null 2.5th to 97.5th percentile 0.448 to 0.547). For every arm in the earlier headline tables (BM25, dense, flat RRF, cross-encoder rerank v2, ANALYST and ANALYST-RERANK-ONLY at H3, 50 candidates) report: (i) session recall@10 of a LENGTH-ONLY ranking (longest sessions first, no query) at H3; (ii) each arm's session recall@10 LENGTH-MATCHED: the arm's ranked list restricted to sessions whose length lies within the gold sessions' length range of that question widened by 25% on each side (gold sessions always kept), so a long distractor cannot be outranked by a short one; for BM25, dense and flat RRF also by re-retrieval inside that matched pool; (iii) the change in each headline number (H3 session recall@10 and the paired differences ANALYST minus RRF and RERANK minus RRF). Descriptive; no pass rule; the point is that no arm is credited for the benchmark artefact. All 94 and the low-overlap 23, content-cluster paired intervals for the changes.

## E6 addendum: size-matched random control and the survival rule (committed before running)

# Reconciliation of baselines reported with different values (claims audit; exact in-process retriever; pilot; raw file results/exploratory/xp_reconcile.json, script emlab.xp_reconcile)

Method: the flat RRF baseline was recomputed at H1, H2, H3 under four definitions: D50 = the 50-round tier-0 candidate list handed to the analyst (fusion of two depth-50 lists), D200 = the registered curve (fusion of two depth-200 lists, output cut at 200 rounds), D400 = the RAG contexts (depth 400), and the first 20 rounds of D400; the packed 4,000-token knowledge-update numbers under each; and the analyst-versus-baseline paired differences under each.

1. RRF session recall@10. H3: curve_pilot.json 0.4754 = D200 (0.4754 recomputed), analyst_full.json 0.473 = D50 (0.4732), D400 0.4807 (the 0.481 seen in the E2 re-run), and the "exact leg" 0.4727 of es_hybrid_pooled.json is a third construction (BM25 top-100 rounds plus the dense leg from the top-100 CHUNKS rolled up to rounds, RRF 60). # ES-pilot: Elasticsearch Basic-features parity pilot (V2 Part E rule-setting pilot; pilot split; NO model call; rule committed before the run)

Scope and ownership: runs only against the node `emlab-es-16096` (ES 9.5.6, the original node) and only on the index `emlab-rounds-v1`, which this owner created and owns (150,526 chunk documents, bbq_hnsw `vector`, `text` with the english analyzer). Nothing is written to any other index, no new index is created, the licence, ML deployments and container settings are untouched, and the separate Basic-licence replication node `emlab-es-rep-16096` built by the replication stream is neither used nor modified. Basic-licence features only: `match` BM25, `knn` on `dense_vector` with `rescore_vector` oversample 3, and client-side fusion; no Elasticsearch `rrf` or `linear` retriever, no inference API, no semantic_text.
Design: pilot split, 94 questions with gold (90 content clusters), levels H0 (own haystack), H1, H2, H3 with the registered pilot sets (`pool_sets_pilot_v2.json`) and level definitions; filters by session id (a `terms` filter for H0 to H2, a `must_not` of the excluded sessions for H3, the same session sets as the exact retriever). Arms: BM25 (ES `match` on `text`, chunk hits rolled up to rounds by max, top 200 rounds), dense (ES kNN, k 600, num_candidates 1,000, `rescore_vector` oversample 3, filter heuristic `acorn` read back; a `fanout` variant as a secondary), RRF (client-side RRF 60 over the two ES round lists, depth 200). The cross-encoder is NOT re-run in this pilot (same model, local); only the overlap of the ES-RRF top-20 head with the exact top-20 head is reported. Comparator: the exact in-process retriever at the registered curve definition (BM25, dense and RRF 60 at depth 200).
Primary metric: session recall@10 (first 10 distinct sessions of the round ranking) per arm and level, paired difference ES minus exact with a 95% content-cluster clustered bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 16096). Descriptive companions: mean top-10 session overlap with the exact ranking, mean round-level top-10 overlap, and the top-20 head overlap for RRF.
PLANNED PARITY RULE for Part E (committed now): replication HOLDS for an arm at a level iff the paired mean difference ES minus exact lies within +/-0.03 AND its 95% interval lies within +/-0.05. The rule is applied to BM25, dense and RRF at each of H0, H1, H2, H3. The ES-pilot's result fixes only whether this rule is kept, tightened or loosened BEFORE V2 is frozen; no Elasticsearch setting may be tuned afterwards to remove a difference (differences are reported). If an arm fails the rule, part E reports the Elasticsearch values separately and the design must not quote the exact-retriever recall as an Elasticsearch result for that arm.
Control that must break parity (sensitivity): the same BM25 queries with a deliberately MISMATCHED query analyzer (the index analyzer stays english; the query uses `analyzer: whitespace`, which keeps case and punctuation and does not stem, and the extreme `analyzer: keyword`, which turns the whole question into one term). The control PASSES (the parity test is shown to be sensitive) iff at H0 the paired difference to the exact retriever for the whitespace variant is at most -0.10 (the parity band is +/-0.03, so a defect of this size must show) and the keyword variant gives session recall@10 at most 0.05 at every level.
Reported per level and arm: the difference, its interval, the verdict of the rule, the heuristic read-back, ES `took` and wall time per query (indicative only, shared host). Controls also reported: a planted needle through Elasticsearch is NOT run here (it needs a write to the index and no other index may be written); the wrong-query comparison is not needed for a parity measurement.
