Group 1 (new): the gold sessions (contents, `emlab.content`) of every pilot question with retrieval gold (94 of 102; the 8 abstention questions have none) that are not yet in the fact pool: 98 distinct contents, for 55 questions (39 questions have all their gold already in the pool). 98 is within the cap of 250, so no selection rule is needed and no question is left out. Group 2 (re-extraction): with the 152 calls that remain, the 30 gold sessions of Stage X0 whose facts exist only at cap 12 (no cap-30 output) are re-extracted at cap 30, so that the gold sessions are not disadvantaged by the cap mismatch of Stage X1: 30 calls. Total 98 + 30 = 128 calls; 122 of the 250 are NOT used (no further call is made). Prompt `extract-v2-cap30` (sha16 `ae055bca99452fb6`) UNCHANGED, one session per call, single pass, same parser and record fields as STAGE-X0.md Section 3, date rule of Stage X0 (earliest date over all occurrences of the content). The 98 new sessions get opaque ids (`emlab.anon.opaque_sid` of the smallest real id); the 30 re-extractions keep their Stage X0 opaque ids and write to a different directory. The runner is blind to gold: new and re-extracted sessions interleave in a seeded order (sha256 of `16096:X2:order:` + id) and nothing in a file marks the group or the question (the key is in `out/stage_x2_key/`, which the runner must not read).

## 2. The enlarged pool and what it does to every number

Pool = the 1,000 sessions of Stage X1 + the 98 new gold sessions = 1,098 sessions (the 30 re-extractions replace the cap-12 fact lists of sessions already in the pool). Gold sessions are now 177 of the 1,098 (16 percent; in the real pool of 18,464 contents the pilot's gold sessions would be about 1 percent), and the pool is still about 6 percent of the real one. Consequences, stated before computing: (a) absolute hit rates are OPTIMISTIC for the real pool (a 6 percent pool) and must not be read as rates; (b) the distractors of one question now include the other questions' gold sessions, which are longer and fact-dense (about 14 to 16 facts per session at cap 30 against about 12 for random sessions), a somewhat harder distractor mix than a random pool of the same size, which makes the comparison between arms slightly conservative for the facts arms (more facts, more chances to outrank a gold fact); (c) pool size effects are shown directly by the scaling view (200 sessions against the full pool, same questions, same code); (d) because the other questions' gold sessions are in the pool, a wrong-query control is meaningful (a question's gold looked up with another question's text).

## 3. The pre-registered test (no model)

Questions: all pilot questions with retrieval gold whose gold sessions are all in the pool (94 expected). Embeddings: `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` as in STAGE-X1.md Section 8 (a fact embedded as its statement; a raw round chunked at 480 tokens, a round's score the maximum over its chunks; "query: " and "passage: "). Arms (fixed): CONTROL `rrf_raw` (RRF, rank constant 60, of BM25 over the raw rounds and dense over the raw rounds); `rrf_facts` (RRF of BM25 over the repaired fact statements and dense over them); `fused_facts_raw` (one list of facts and rounds: RRF of the two lists above, items of both kinds in one ranking).
PRIMARY METRIC: session-level hit at top 10: items are pooled per session by best (max-score) position, and a question is a HIT if one of its gold sessions is among the first 10 sessions. Secondary: item-level hit at top 10; the best gold session's rank. Paired difference of each facts arm against the control (mean of the per-question hit difference) with a 95 percent interval by CONTENT CLUSTER (clustered paired bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, seed 16096), wins and losses reported. Strata: weak-list (11), other low-overlap (12), knowledge-update (15), the rest (56); and the union of the two low-overlap strata (23); by question type (temporal-reasoning 26, multi-session 23, single-session-user 12, single-session-assistant 12, knowledge-update 15, single-session-preference 6). Wrong-query control: each question's gold looked up with the text of the next question in the list (skipping one with shared gold); the mean session hit at top 10 per arm. Scaling: the 39 questions whose gold is entirely in the Stage X0 200 sessions, at the 200-session pool and at the full pool, same code.
THRESHOLDS (fixed now; they come from the 11-question result of STAGE-X1.md 8.1, the hard subset on which the facts arms gained 3 to 4 questions at top 10, and are therefore SMALLER here, where most questions are easy): the facts list is worth carrying forward to the design of a confirmatory test iff, for at least one facts arm against the control, ALL of: (D1, do no harm) on all 94 questions the paired mean difference in session hit at top 10 is at least -0.02 and its cluster lower bound at least -0.05; (D2, benefit where retrieval fails) on the 23 low-overlap questions the paired mean difference is at least +0.15 and the arm wins at least 3 more questions than it loses; (D3) the wrong-query hit rate of the arm is at most 0.05. If not, the facts list is not carried forward on this evidence. What the test can detect: with 94 paired questions and about 15 to 25 percent of them discordant between two arms, the standard error of the mean difference is about 0.04 to 0.05, so a difference smaller than about 0.11 to 0.15 (80 percent power) cannot be resolved on all 94; for the 23 low-overlap questions it is about 0.3, for strata of 11 to 15 questions 0.4 or more. The expected effect, if the 11-question gain held and nothing else changed, is about +0.03 to +0.04 on all 94 (11 of 94 questions at about +0.3): below what the test can detect. D1 is therefore a no-harm screen, not a test of benefit, and D2 (+0.15 on 23 questions, below its detectable size of 0.3) is a screen: passing means only that a larger confirmatory test is worth designing; failing says the 11-question gain did not generalise to the other low-overlap questions.

## 4. Research note: the three signals named as missing (smallest experiments)

(1) TIME ANCHORING. The pilot has 26 temporal-reasoning questions of 94. The registered deterministic rule (`emlab.timeparse.parse_question_range`: absolute dates, then month and year, then relative expressions such as "a week ago", "last Saturday") resolves a date range for only 9 of the 94 questions (6 of the 26 temporal, 2 multi-session, 1 single-session-user); the other 20 temporal questions ask about order, duration or counts between events ("which came first", "how many days between"), which a question-date filter cannot resolve. So deterministic date resolution can at best help the 6 resolvable temporal questions. Registered EXPLORATORY arm (it needs only existing data: the question text and question date, and a date for every session): for the questions whose range resolves, restrict the candidate sessions to those whose date falls inside the range widened by 3 days on both sides, then rank with each of the three arms; session date = the date in the question's own haystack when the session is there (gold sessions always), otherwise the stored date of the session (the earliest date over occurrences). Reported: how many questions resolve (by type), the share whose gold session is inside the window, the mean number of pool sessions inside the window (selectivity), and the session hit at top 10 with and without the filter for each arm. Not run: a soft date-proximity feature fused with the arms (the next step if the hard filter helps), and any model-based date reasoning. This arm cannot change D1 to D3.
(2) USER PREFERENCES. From the facts and sessions already extracted, for the 6 single-session-preference questions: the share of the rubric's content words found in the gold sessions' USER turns against ASSISTANT turns; the user-authored share of the gold sessions' facts and how many facts overlapping the rubric by at least 2 words are user-authored against assistant-authored. A high assistant share would mean the "preference" content is mostly assistant-stated and a user-preference instruction has little to extract; a high user share would mean it is user-stated but not extracted as facts. Exploratory, cheap, no model.
(3) CROSS-SESSION AGGREGATION. For the 23 multi-session questions: the share with ANY gold session in the top 10 sessions beside ALL gold sessions in the top 10, 20 and 50 sessions, and the mean fraction of gold sessions in the top 10 and 50, per arm. The test of Stage X1 scored only the first gold item and so overstated what aggregation questions need. Exploratory; part of the scorer, no extra calls.

7.5 What the pass does and does not mean (stated, the rule is not changed). 7.7 Research notes (exploratory, no model).
(1) Time anchoring. 9 of 94 questions resolve a range (6 of 26 temporal). The hard date filter (range +/- 3 days) raised session hit at 10 on those 9 from 0.333 (raw) and 0.444 (facts, fused) to 0.778 for all three arms (7 of 9). Gold was inside the window for 7 of 9; the filter lost one question (311778f1, gold outside the window) and gained 4 to 5 depending on the arm. All 6 resolvable temporal questions hit with the filter against 1 to 2 without. Mean window = 124 of 1,098 sessions (11 percent); in a real 18k pool the window would hold roughly 10 times more sessions, so the gain is optimistic and selectivity must be re-measured. The other 20 temporal questions (order, duration, counts) are not reachable by a date filter. Next step if pursued: a soft date-proximity feature, because 2 of 9 gold sessions fell outside the hard window.
(2) User preferences (6 questions). User turns hold 33 percent of the rubric word hits pooled (by question 0.19 to 0.55). Of the gold sessions' 78 facts, 33 are user-authored; facts overlapping the rubric by 2+ words are 16 user-authored against 13 assistant-authored. So the preference content is mostly assistant-stated or mixed and is extracted as facts of both authors; the facts do not isolate the user's preference. One question (54026fce) has no gold facts (missing record). Preference is also where rrf_facts loses (W1 L2): the facts arm is not good for it. Small n.
(3) Cross-session aggregation (23 multi-session, mean 2.6 gold sessions). ANY gold session in the top 10: raw 0.870, rrf_facts 0.957, fused 0.913. ALL gold sessions in the top 10: 0.609, 0.565, 0.609; top 20: 0.739, 0.696, 0.783; top 50: 0.783 for all three. Mean fraction of gold sessions in the top 10: 0.734, 0.788, 0.790; top 50: 0.909, 0.907, 0.923. So the first-gold hit overstates what aggregation needs by about 0.3 to 0.4 at top 10; the facts lists help find one session but do not help find all of them; 22 percent of aggregation questions still miss at least one gold session in the top 50.

7.8 Recommendation. By the pre-set rules, carry rrf_facts forward to the DESIGN of a confirmatory test (not a deployment decision, and not evidence of benefit on unseen low-overlap questions, see 7.5a). Design points: use the untouched test split, not the pilot; primary comparison on low-overlap questions selected by a rule on the control's failure fixed before the data; include fused_facts_raw as the arm with zero observed session-hit losses in this pilot; size the low-overlap stratum from the other-12 result (no change for rrf_facts, but these questions were seen in X1) so a +0.15 effect is resolvable (roughly 60 or more questions); report pool-size scaling; treat preference and any-versus-all aggregation as separate pre-registered questions; keep the date filter as an exploratory soft feature. If that budget is not available, the honest reading is: rrf_facts passed the registered harm-tolerance screen D1 (observed 4 session-hit losses and 7 gains; the interval for the overall effect, [-0.032, +0.104], does not exclude a loss), and the observed gains are concentrated in questions the retrievers already failed on and that were seen before; fused_facts_raw had no observed losses.
