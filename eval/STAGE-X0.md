# STAGE X0: extraction canary on the pilot split (design and rules, committed before any call)

Status: PILOT stage, never confirmatory. Approved by the user for this stage only: an extraction canary on the PILOT split, exactly 200 headless calls, one fact-extraction call per session (user message of 2026-11-16 "Yes, run canary", relayed by main; an approval for this stage is not approval for the 6,100-call or 22,500-call steps of PREREGISTRATION-V2.md Section 14). Hard split: pilot only (test, held_back and dev2 stay untouched). The eval owner prepares the inputs and scores; the phasef owner runs the calls; no model call is made by the eval owner and the eval owner does not read any result before the scorer is committed. No judge anywhere in this stage: every score is mechanical.

Purpose, in this order: (1) measure time, input and output tokens and notional cost per extraction call, so the 6,100 and 22,500 call steps can be priced; (2) fact quality by mechanical checks only; (3) a first look at whether extracted facts could reach what retrieval missed.

## 1. The 200 sessions (rule fixed here, before the list is built)

Unit: a session CONTENT (sha256 of the ordered role and text list, as in `emlab.content`); the 19,195 ids collapse to 18,464 contents. The 200 sessions are 200 distinct contents. Opaque id: `emlab.anon.opaque_sid` of the content's smallest real id (the real id never reaches the runner). The runner sees only the opaque id, the date and the turns (role, content; the dataset's `has_answer` flags are removed).

Groups, in this order (a content already taken by an earlier group stays there):
1. weak_list: ALL gold sessions of the pilot questions (with retrieval gold) whose depth-300 Stage G candidate list held NO gold session by the gold labels. By that definition there are 11 questions (the 10 with an accepted A-DIG and A-REORDER result plus one whose result was rejected; the 10 of the coordinator's summary are the accepted ones; the rule uses all 11).
2. low_overlap: all gold sessions of the OTHER whole-pool low-overlap pilot questions (the 23 of `lowoverlap_pool_h3.json` minus the weak-list ones), taken in question-id order up to a cap of 40 sessions (the cap is stated now; the expected count is below it, so it is not expected to bind).
3. knowledge_update: all gold sessions of the 15 pilot knowledge-update questions (non-abstention) not already taken.
4. random_non_gold: the rest of the 200, a seeded random sample (seed `16096:X0:sample`) of NON-GOLD sessions: contents that appear in a PILOT haystack and are not the gold content of ANY question of the dataset (so no test or dev2 gold content is extracted as filler), stratified by session length. Length = total characters of the turns. Strata = the quintiles of the whole 18,464-content pool (equal shares, 20 percent each), so the sample's cost per call is representative of the pool; the sample size per stratum is the largest-remainder share of the remaining slots. The length distribution of the sample is reported against the whole pool and against the pilot candidates (`prep_report.json`).

Disclosures: gold content of a pilot question can be byte-identical to gold content of a test question (399 of 940 gold ids have a twin); extracting it touches content the test split also contains. This is pilot work on pilot questions; the sensitivity analysis registered for the test split (questions whose gold content appears in a pilot haystack) already covers it, and no extraction result is a test result.

Date of a session: ONE date per session, chosen by the rule "the earliest date over all occurrences of the content in the dataset" (dates in the dataset differ between questions for the same content; the runner's earlier rule was the earliest date seen, and this is the same rule made explicit). It is written in the header exactly as the dataset spells it (`%Y/%m/%d (%a) %H:%M`). Effect: the date is used only by the model to resolve relative dates into `event_time`; this stage does no event_time-dependent scoring (event_time is only reported as present or null), so the rule cannot change any score here. It may change event_time values for twins that occur with a later date in their own question; part X's linking would need the per-question date and is not tested here.

Run order: sorted by sha256(`16096:X0:order:` + opaque id), written to `run_order.json`; gold and non-gold interleave and the first 10 are a random 10 of the 200 (a fair canary for time and tokens). The call counter is the runner's own and stops at 200 calls; no retries.

## 2. Extraction (unchanged from the phasef stream)

Prompt version `extract-v2-anon`: `EXTRACT_SYSTEM` plus `EXTRACT_V2_TEMPLATE` of `phasef/prompts.py` (sha256 of system + "\n" + template begins `a2336c7976bb8fc2`, checked by the eval owner against phasef commit 6dd36b9), UNCHANGED, including its "Below are 1 chat sessions" wording, one session per call, no batching. Rendering (`phasef.facts.render_for_extraction`): `### SESSION id=<opaque id> date=<date>` followed by the rounds `[r0] ...`, a round starting at each user turn, round text `role: content` lines. Facts as emitted: subject, slot, cardinality, predicate, object, value, unit, event_time, modality, scope, author, round (integer), quote (at most 12 words), cue_keys; the runner composes `round_id` = `<opaque session>:<round index>`; at most 12 facts per session (the prompt's cap: long sessions cannot yield more, and the scorer reports the share at the cap). Parser: `parse_extraction_v2` repairs missing closing brackets (status `repaired`, counted separately); anything else is `parse_failure`; no retry. Nothing in the prompt or the files names a question, a level, a split or a gold flag.

## 3. Runner records (fields)

Directory on t2-dev: inputs `~/emlab-16096/out/stage_x0/sessions/<opaque id>.json` (`session_id`, `date`, `turns` = list of {role, content}) and `out/stage_x0/run_order.json`; the runner must NOT read `out/stage_x0_key/` (the key, with groups and gold membership, is for the scorer). Results: `~/emlab-16096/in/stage_x0/<opaque id>.json` with fields: `session_id`, `order_index`, `status` (`ok`, `repaired`, `parse_failure`, `rejected` with `reject_reason`, `call_error`, `not_run`), `prompt_version` (`extract-v2-anon`), `prompt_sha16`, `model`, `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, `seconds` (call latency), `cost_usd` (notional, from the runner's price table; `price_table` named in the run file), `dropped` (parser drops), `facts` (list of the emitted facts with `session_id` = the opaque id, `round` integer and `round_id`), `raw_output_path`. Failures stay in the denominators; a missing record counts as a failure.

## 4. Scoring (`emlab.stage_x0_score`, committed before any result is read; mechanical, no judge)

1. Time, input and output tokens (and cache kinds) and notional cost per call: distributions overall and by length stratum; Spearman of characters against input tokens and against seconds.
2. Facts per session against length (distribution, Spearman, by stratum, by group); the share of sessions at the 12-fact cap and with zero facts; parse failures, repairs, rejections and call errors counted.
3. Quote-and-speaker check: the committed phasef checker (`phasef/provenance.py` at commit 27a4c64, vendored unchanged as `emlab/provenance_x04.py`, sha256 `adde67b4...`): (a) the quote occurs verbatim (whitespace collapsed, lower-cased) in the cited round; (b) the stated author equals the speaker (pass, mismatch, AMBIGUOUS counted separately, undefined); Wilson interval; the checker's own controls (changed word, adjacent round, flipped author) are re-run on this data and must reject 100 percent. Reference: that checker's committed "useful as a write gate" line was at least 95 percent of facts passing (a); here it is reported against, not decided.
4. Gold-answer heuristic (CRUDE, labelled so, not a judge): for each question of the three gold groups, whether any extracted fact's quote, subject, predicate or object contains the gold answer string, or its key tokens (every number of the answer if it has numbers, otherwise at least 60 percent of its content tokens including one of five or more characters); beside it the same test on the raw text of the question's gold sessions (the ceiling: an answer that is not in the raw text cannot be in a fact). Split by weak_list, low_overlap and knowledge_update. Answers that are not literal strings (preference rubrics, counts that need adding) are expected to miss; this is a floor, not a recall measure.
5. Weak-list toy lookup, NO model: for each of the 11 weak-list questions, BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75, the registered tokeniser) over the extracted fact statements (subject predicate object) of the 200 sessions; does the top 10 contain a fact from a gold session of that question. Secondary variant: statement plus quote plus cue keys. Control: the same BM25 over the raw round text of the same 200 sessions (is a gold-session round in the top 10). This is a TOY pool of 200 sessions, not the whole pool: it reports only "the fact text is findable when present", never reach in the real pool, and a raw-round hit in the toy pool says nothing about the 300-candidate failure of the real pool.
6. Extrapolation to 6,100 and 22,500 calls: from the RANDOM non-gold sample only (equal-share length strata), a stratified estimator of per-call seconds, input tokens, output tokens and notional cost with a 95 percent interval by resampling within strata (2,000 resamples, seed 16096); totals = per-call mean x N; wall time at the observed throughput of this run (wall seconds per call, which includes the run's concurrency) and sequential hours; the range is that interval, plus the stated caveats: the run's concurrency, rate limits and the loaded host are not scaled, the linking calls of part X are not priced here, and the 22,500 figure includes sessions longer than the pilot candidates only through the strata.

## 5. What each outcome changes, and what the result cannot show (stated before the run)

- Cost and time: the extrapolated totals replace the unmeasured cost line of PREREGISTRATION-V2.md Section 14 and Section 12; if the whole-pool extraction (about 22,500 calls) is extrapolated above what the user would approve, part X is reported as unaffordable as designed and V2 changes it to a cheaper pilot (X-a only) or drops it.
- Quality: provenance (a) below 0.90 or any control not rejected means the extraction output cannot yet be trusted as a write gate and part X needs a prompt revision first; between 0.90 and 0.95 is reported against the 0.95 line. A cap share above 0.5 means the 12-fact cap is binding for long sessions and V2 must say so.
- First look: if the gold answer appears in extracted facts for few of the weak-list questions, the facts layer as prompted is not a route to those questions; if it does appear, that shows only that the extraction can carry the answer, not that it can be found in the real pool.
- NOT shown: any retrieval or answer result; anything about the whole pool (the lookup pool is 200 sessions, a toy); extraction quality on sessions not sampled; linking, supersession or knowledge-update behaviour (no linking calls are made); the effect of batching or of a different prompt; behaviour on test sessions. Nothing here freezes or changes a V2 threshold by itself; main decides what to write.

### 7.3 Facts per session and the 12-fact cap
Mean 9.53 facts per session (median 10; 0 to 12). 56 of 200 sessions (28 percent) are at the cap of 12, 3 have zero facts. The cap share rises with length: 0 percent (stratum 1), 8, 26, 34, 43 percent (stratum 5); Spearman of characters against fact count 0.51. By group: weak_list 9 of 20 at the cap, low_overlap 15 of 33, knowledge_update 15 of 26 (39 of 79 gold sessions, 49 percent) against 17 of 121 random sessions (14 percent); mean facts per session 10.8, 10.9, 11.2 for the gold groups against 8.57 for the random ones. No-model coverage check: capped sessions cite 89 percent of their rounds on average and all of them have a fact in the last third of the session (uncapped 83 percent and 95 percent), so the model spreads the 12 facts over the whole session rather than stopping early; what the cap removes is a selection among candidate facts, which this run cannot count.
What the cap implies. The sessions that carry gold are long and dense and are capped about half the time, so the cap is binding exactly where it matters; for the random pool it binds for about one session in seven (and for 43 percent of the longest quintile). Facts lost to the cap are not counted anywhere in this run. Suggestive evidence (post-hoc, not in the committed scorer): the two gold questions whose answer is in the raw session text but in no extracted fact each involve capped sessions (fca70973: its one gold session has 12 facts; gpt4_ab202e7f: 3 of its 5 gold sessions have 12 facts and the others 10 and 11). Smallest check, which needs the user's approval because it is a new model stage: re-extract 10 capped sessions (the one fca70973 session, two gpt4_ab202e7f sessions and seven other capped gold sessions) with a cap of 30 (only the "At most 12 facts" sentence changed, new version string `extract-v2-cap30`): 10 calls, about 0.8 USD notional and about 2 to 3 minutes at concurrency 2; count the facts beyond 12, whether the missing answer then appears, and how output tokens grow (the cost model above assumes the cap).

By group: weak_list 0.954, low_overlap 0.958, knowledge_update 0.952, random 0.964. 147 of 197 sessions have every quote passing. (b) Among the 1,829 facts passing (a): 1,806 speaker pass, 22 AMBIGUOUS (the quote occurs in turns of both speakers; counted separately and not a pass), 1 mismatch. ### 7.7 What the result shows and does not show (Section 5 applied; none of it freezes a V2 threshold)
Shows: the extraction call is mechanically reliable at this volume (200 of 200 parsed, 0 repairs); one call costs about 15.5k input and 1.9k output tokens, about 15.7 s and 0.077 USD notional (about 0.034 USD in the derived direct-API estimate); the whole-pool extraction of about 22,500 calls is extrapolated at about 1,630 USD notional and 49 hours of wall time at concurrency 2 (derived direct-API estimate about 753 USD), the 6,100-call step at about 442 USD and 13 hours (derived about 204 USD), before the tail of the longest sessions and before linking; the quote check passes the 95 percent reference and every control; facts carry the answer for 30 of the 32 gold questions whose raw text has it; and the cap is binding for about half of the gold sessions.
Does NOT show: any retrieval or answer result; that facts can reach what retrieval missed in the real pool (the toy pool has 200 sessions, 79 of them gold); extraction quality on sessions not sampled or on the longest sessions; the number of facts lost to the cap; any effect of linking or supersession (no linking calls were made); that the heuristic's misses are extraction misses rather than non-literal answers; anything about the test split. Decisions for main and the user: whether the extrapolated volume is acceptable, whether to run the 10-call cap check, and whether to repair the 4 percent of quotes not found in the cited round (and the 14.6 percent over the word limit) before any volume.
