# STAGE X1: extraction at volume and the scale test of the facts-as-candidates route (design and rules, committed before any call)

Status: PILOT stage, never confirmatory, exactly 800 headless calls, one fact-extraction call per session. Approved by the user for this stage only (user message of 2026-11-16 "Yes, run it", relayed by main; it is not approval for the 6,100-call or 22,500-call steps). Hard split: pilot-haystack sessions only; test, held_back and dev2 untouched. The eval owner prepares the inputs and scores; the phasef owner runs the calls; the eval owner makes no model call and reads no result before the scorer and the decision rule (this file) are committed. No judge: every score is mechanical.

The only decision this stage makes: whether the facts-as-candidates route is worth asking the user for the 6,100-call step (Section 4). Everything else is descriptive.

## 1. The 800 sessions (rule fixed here, before the list is built)

Unit: a session content (sha256 of ordered role and text; `emlab.content`). Candidates: contents that appear in a PILOT haystack, are not the gold content of ANY question of the dataset, and are not among the 200 sessions already extracted in Stage X0 (about 4,200 contents). Of the 800:
- 40 LONG sessions: the 40 longest candidates by characters (5 percent of the stage), taken deliberately so that the cost of long sessions is measured; flagged `long` in the key and EXCLUDED from the main cost extrapolation (they are reported separately).
- 760 RANDOM sessions: 152 from each of the five whole-pool length quintiles (edges 4,935, 8,579, 12,051 and 15,112 characters; equal shares, i.e. stratified to the whole-pool length distribution), a seeded draw (`16096:X1:sample`, Python `random.Random(...).shuffle` over the ids sorted ascending) from the candidates that are not in the long set.
Opaque ids only (`emlab.anon.opaque_sid` of the content's smallest real id); the runner learns neither the stratum nor the long flag (the key is in `out/stage_x1_key/`, which the runner must not read). Date rule as in Stage X0: the earliest date over all occurrences of the content in the dataset, in the dataset's own spelling. Run order: sorted by sha256(`16096:X1:order:` + opaque id), so long, random and all strata interleave. Calls: 800, hard cap, no retries; a failed or missing record stays in every denominator.

## 2. Extraction

Prompt `extract-v2-cap30` (sha16 `ae055bca99452fb6`), UNCHANGED, single pass, one session per call, the same parser and record fields as STAGE-X0.md Section 3 (opaque session id, integer round, round_id, quote, author, event_time, subject, slot, cardinality, predicate, object, value, unit, modality, scope, cue_keys; at most 30 facts per session). Results: `~/emlab-16096/in/stage_x1/<opaque id>.json` and `_run.json`; inputs `out/stage_x1/sessions/<opaque id>.json` and `out/stage_x1/run_order.json`.

## 3. The 1,000-session fact pool, mechanical post-processing, and the cap mismatch

Pool = the 800 new sessions (cap 30) plus the 200 sessions of Stage X0. Treatment of the cap mismatch (disclosed): for the X0 sessions the pool uses the cap-30 output where one exists and the cap-12 output otherwise. Cap-30 outputs exist for 20 X0 sessions (the 10 of Stage X0B, and the arm-S outputs of Stage X0C, which add 10 more; for the sessions with two the X0B output is used); the arm-P outputs (a different prompt) are not used. So 180 X0 sessions enter at cap 12, not 190. The scorer reports how many of the 79 gold sessions are at cap 12 without a cap-30 output. Direction of the bias: the gold sessions are mostly at cap 12 (fewer facts per session than the 800 cap-30 distractors), which gives the distractors more chances to outrank a gold fact and makes the test slightly CONSERVATIVE for the facts arm.
Mechanical post-processing, fixed now (it needs no model call; `emlab.stage_x1_score.repair_fact`): (a) a missing author field is filled from the speaker of the turn containing the quote when that speaker is unique (otherwise `unknown`); a model author that is present is kept; (b) quote repair: a quote verbatim in the cited round is `verbatim`; equal to the cited round after removing punctuation, or every ellipsis-separated part found in order there, is `punct_cited` (accepted as grounded); verbatim in another round of the session is `other_round` (re-pointed to that round); a best token coverage of at least 0.8 in some round is `paraphrase` (flagged); anything else is `ungrounded`. The REPAIRED pool keeps verbatim, punct_cited and other_round facts and DROPS paraphrase and ungrounded facts; the RAW pool keeps all. Both quote-check rates (raw: verbatim in the cited round; repaired: grounded after repair) are reported for the 800 new outputs.

## 4. The primary test and the decision rule (fixed before any call)

Questions: the 11 weak-list pilot questions (the ones whose depth-300 candidate list held no gold session by the gold labels; the same 11 as STAGE-X0.md). Their gold sessions are all in the pool (20 sessions, from Stage X0).
Facts arm: BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75, the registered tokeniser) over the fact statements (subject, predicate, object) of the REPAIRED 1,000-session pool, one document per fact; a question is a HIT if a fact of one of its gold sessions is among the top 10 facts. Control: BM25 over the raw round text (`role: content` lines of each round) of the SAME 1,000 sessions, one document per round; a HIT if a gold-session round is among the top 10 rounds. Same query (the question text), same tokeniser.
DECISION RULE: the facts arm is at least as good as the raw control (facts hits at least raw hits, over the 11 questions) AND the facts arm has at least 6 of 11 hits: then the 6,100-call step is worth asking the user for; otherwise the facts-as-candidates route stops before volume. The decision uses only the statement variant, the repaired pool and the 1,000-session pool; the statement plus quote plus cue keys variant is a registered secondary and cannot change the verdict.
Reported descriptively (none changes the verdict): the same two arms for the 12 other low-overlap questions and the 15 knowledge-update questions whose gold sessions are in the pool; the rank of the first gold fact (and first gold round) per question; a COMBINED arm (the design would use both): a capped list of 10 items made of the 5 best facts and the 5 best raw rounds, a HIT if any item is from a gold session; a WRONG-QUERY control (each weak-list question's gold looked up with the text of the next weak-list question; hits should be near zero); the raw pool (no repair) beside the repaired pool; and how the 200-session toy result of Stage X0 (7 of 11 facts against 5 of 11 raw, with cap-12 facts, no repair) changes at 1,000, computed with the same code on the 200-session pool and the 1,000-session pool.
What 11 questions can and cannot show: with 11 questions a hit count of 6 against 5 is not a resolvable difference (a count of 11 questions has a binomial standard error of about 0.15 in the proportion); the test can only show that the facts route is not obviously worse than raw text and not obviously failing at this size, not that it is better, and the 11 questions are the ones selected because the retrievers missed them, so they are a deliberately hard and small sample. A 1,000-session pool is 5 percent of the real pool of 18,464 contents (an 18-fold increase would still lie ahead); passing here is a necessary condition for asking, not evidence that the route works at the whole pool; failing is informative (the toy result would not survive a modest increase of the pool).

### 7.7 What the result shows and does not show
Shows: at a pool of 1,000 sessions (5 percent of the real pool), BM25 over one-pass extracted fact statements finds a gold-session fact in the top 10 for only 2 of the 11 weak-list questions, against 1 of 11 for raw rounds; the pre-set rule (at least as good as raw AND at least 6 of 11) is not met, and every arm loses two thirds or more of its hits when the pool grows from 200 to 1,000 sessions, so a larger pool would be expected to do no better; facts rank the first gold item higher than raw text for 9 of 11 questions but mostly outside the top 10; the extraction itself is operationally sound at volume (800 of 800 parsed, 0.991 of quotes grounded after repair, 0.466 user share).
Does NOT show: that facts cannot help in any form (the test is lexical BM25 over statements, one pass, no embeddings, no linking, no reranking, no query expansion, no reader); anything about the 12 other low-overlap or 15 knowledge-update questions beyond the descriptive counts (the latter are all found by every arm); a rate (11 questions are a small, deliberately hard sample and 2 against 1 is not a resolvable difference); behaviour at the whole pool of 18,464 sessions; the effect of the cap mismatch beyond the stated direction (30 of 79 gold sessions at cap 12). Pilot, never confirmatory; nothing here freezes or changes a V2 threshold by itself; main decides what to write.

## 8. POST-HOC EXPLORATORY follow-up on the existing pool (rules and thresholds written before anything below was computed; no model call except the LOCAL E5 embedding model on CPU; it cannot reverse the stop decision of Section 7.1, it only says whether a different, pre-registered test would be worth designing)

Code: `emlab.xp_x1_dense`. Pool: exactly the repaired 1,000-session pool of Section 3 (its 200-session part is the toy pool); the raw rounds of the same sessions. Embeddings: `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` (the model used for the round index), mean pooling, "query: " for questions and "passage: " for documents; a fact is embedded as its statement (subject, predicate, object); a raw round is chunked at 480 tokens as in the round index and a round's score is the maximum over its chunks. Questions: the 11 weak-list questions (primary), the 12 other low-overlap and the 15 knowledge-update questions (descriptive). Arms (item level; an item is a fact or a round; a HIT is an item of a gold session): bm25_facts and bm25_raw (the Section 4 arms, repeated), dense_facts, dense_raw (control), rrf_facts (RRF, rank constant 60, of bm25_facts and dense_facts), rrf_raw (control: RRF of bm25_raw and dense_raw), fused_facts_raw (one list: RRF, rank constant 60, of the rrf_facts list and the rrf_raw list, items of both kinds in one ranking). Reported per arm: top-10 and top-50 hits, the first gold item's rank (median and quartiles over the questions that have one in the list), the SESSION view (items pooled per session by best position, so the rank of the best gold session among the pool's sessions, with top-10 and top-50 session hits), the wrong-query control of each arm (each weak-list question's gold looked up with the next weak-list question's text), and every arm at 200 sessions and at 1,000 with the same code.
WORTH DESIGNING A NEW TEST iff, for at least one facts arm and its raw control (dense_facts against dense_raw; rrf_facts against rrf_raw; fused_facts_raw against rrf_raw), ALL of: (W1) on the 11 weak-list questions at 1,000 sessions the facts arm has at least 3 more top-10 hits than its control; (W2) the facts arm has at least 6 of 11 at top 50 and a median first-gold rank at most half of its control's; (W3) its wrong-query control is at most 1 of 11 at top 10. Otherwise the answer is no. Caveat fixed now: with 11 questions a margin of 3 (for example 4 against 1) is NOT statistically resolvable (if all three discordant questions favour the facts arm, the exact one-sided sign test gives p of about 0.13), so meeting W1 would justify designing a test with a larger question set, not claiming an effect; the criteria are a screen, not a result.
Reading (written after the numbers, a few lines): whether the unreachable-question problem is a candidate-generation problem that single-session facts do not solve in any of these forms, and which kind of missing signal the data suggest (cross-session aggregation, user preferences never stated as facts, questions whose wording shares nothing with the session), using the question types of the 11 questions and the per-question ranks.

### 8.2 Reading (a few lines)
- The 11 questions are 6 temporal-reasoning, 3 single-session-preference and 2 multi-session. Dense and fused retrieval over single-session facts do help (4 to 5 of 11 at top 10 against 0 to 1 for the raw arms, 8 of 11 at top 50 for the fused list), so the unreachable-question problem is partly a candidate-generation problem that single-session facts can solve, but only in the dense or fused forms and only for a minority: three questions stay outside the top 50 even in the fused list (07b6f563 preference, gpt4_4929293b and gpt4_8279ba03 temporal, best first-gold ranks 76, 221 and 329 in the fused list).
- What the data suggest is still missing: (1) time anchoring for the temporal-reasoning questions (6 of 11): wording like "a week ago" or "the last time" shares nothing with the session text and facts carry an event time on only about 10 percent of facts, so a date-aware signal (session dates, resolved event times) is the likeliest missing piece; two of the three never-reached questions are of this kind; (2) preferences never stated as facts for the preference questions (3 of 11; their gold rank is poor in every arm, fca70973 reaches the top 10 only through raw RRF and the fused list); (3) cross-session aggregation for the two multi-session questions: dense facts bring both into the top 10 (rank 1 and 3), but a count or list question needs ALL its gold sessions and this test scores only the FIRST gold item, so it overstates what is reached.
- Caution: all of this is exploratory on 11 hard questions; it justifies designing a larger, pre-registered test of dense and fused fact retrieval with a time-aware component, not a claim that facts work.
