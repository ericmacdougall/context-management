TWENTY SESSIONS GIVE A DIRECTION, NOT A RATE.

Questions: (1) Is single-pass extraction stable when the identical prompt is run twice (the gate set in STAGE-X0B.md Section 7.7 and PREREGISTRATION-V2.md X-G5 asked for at least 85 percent; here a first pilot reading)? (2) Does a one-sentence preference instruction recover the user's own stated preferences and interests (the one prompt gap found in STAGE-X0B.md Section 7.8) without displacing other facts or damaging quote and speaker accuracy?

I keep the requested split because the preference arm tests the one defect actually found, and a stability reading from 10 pairs is enough to say whether the pre-set 85 percent is plausible; if the pooled stability lands between 0.80 and 0.90 it is inconclusive and the proposed next step is 20 fresh pairs (40 calls).

## 4. Scoring (`emlab.stage_x0c_score`, committed before any result is read; mechanical)

1. STABILITY (identical prompt, the 10 replicate pairs: the Stage X0B output against arm S): facts reproduced by quote match (normalised quotes equal, or one contains the other with the shorter at least 8 characters), BOTH directions: stability = (facts of run 1 found in run 2 + facts of run 2 found in run 1) / (facts in run 1 + facts in run 2). Reported pooled over the facts, per session, and as the mean of per-session rates with a Wilson interval by session (n = 10 sessions). Pre-set gate: pooled stability at least 0.85 AND no session below 0.60. The first cap-30 outputs of the other 10 sessions are compared with their cap-12 outputs and labelled (cap change confounded).
2. ARM P against arm S on the same session: user-authored facts (count and share of all facts), total facts, sessions at the cap of 30, facts displaced (arm S facts with no quote match in arm P) and, on the 10 replicate sessions, the EXCESS displacement (displaced share minus the share of the same arm-S facts that were not reproduced by the earlier identical-prompt run), the quote-and-speaker check (vendored phasef checker) on arm P facts and arm S facts, and for the user-authored facts the speaker verdict among those passing (a); tokens, seconds and cost per call, paired, arm P against arm S.
3. fca70973 wish list: the scorer writes the user-authored facts of the fca70973 gold session in both arms to `out/stage_x0c_reading/fca70973_user_facts.json`; the eval owner reads them and records, per item (thrill rides; unique food experiences; nighttime shows), whether a user-authored fact states it (present or absent). No string heuristic is used.

## 5. What counts as "the preference line is safe to adopt" (fixed before the run; ALL must hold)

A1. More user content: the mean per-session increase in user-authored facts, arm P over arm S, is at least +2.0, and arm P has more user-authored facts in at least 14 of the 20 sessions.
A2. Accuracy not damaged: the quote check on arm P facts passes (a) at a rate of at least 0.95, and among arm P's user-authored facts that pass (a) the speaker check passes at least 0.95 (the user line must not make the model attribute assistant text to the user).
A3. Little displacement: arm S facts with no match in arm P are at most 25 percent overall, and on the 10 replicate sessions the excess displacement over the identical-prompt non-reproduction is at most 10 percentage points.
A4. Cap and cost: arm P at the cap of 30 in at most 2 of the 20 sessions, and output tokens per call at most 30 percent above arm S.
A5. The target is hit: at least 2 of the 3 fca70973 wish-list items are present as user-authored facts in arm P and more than in arm S.
If all hold, the preference line is recommended for the V2 draft's part X as a new version string to re-gate (it is still a 20-session pilot: never an adoption without the gate-set check); if any fails, it is not adopted as worded and the failing criterion says what to change. The stability gate (item 1) is reported separately: met means single-pass extraction is stable enough to proceed to the gate-set X-G5 check; not met means a second pass or verification step is added before volume.

## 6. What the stage cannot show

A rate for the pool (20 sessions, 10 replicate pairs); stability of extraction for sessions other than these (the 10 replicate sessions are all capped, long, mostly gold sessions); the effect of the preference line on retrieval or answers; whether the extra user facts are the RIGHT ones beyond the one fca70973 reading; behaviour at the volume of 22,500 calls.

## 8. Results (eval owner; scored with `emlab.stage_x0c_score`, committed in f040706 and fixed in 55a2722 before any result was read). PILOT, MECHANICAL. TWENTY SESSIONS GIVE A DIRECTION, NOT A RATE

Scorer fix 55a2722 (made after the first scoring run crashed and before any result was read): one arm-S record (session s827f306f0106, 19 facts) has NO author field on any fact (the model omitted it); such facts are labelled `author_missing` (never a mismatch or a pass) and A1 is reported both as written and without that session. ### 8.1 Stability gate (identical prompt `extract-v2-cap30`, 10 replicate pairs: the Stage X0B output against arm S)
Pooled share of facts reproduced by quote match, both directions: 0.776 (session mean 0.783; Wilson interval by session [0.474, 0.935], n = 10). Per session: 0.82, 0.79, 0.69, 0.51, 0.93, 0.88, 0.90, 0.71, 0.84, 0.76; the lowest is 0.51 (the fca70973 gold session, 15 and 24 facts in the two runs). Pre-set gate: pooled at least 0.85 and no session below 0.60. VERDICT: FAIL (pooled 0.776 is below the pre-declared inconclusive band of 0.80 to 0.90, and one session is below 0.60); the interval is wide enough that the true value could be near 0.85, which 10 sessions cannot exclude. The 10 non-replicate sessions (first cap-30 output against their cap-12 output; NOT an identical-prompt comparison, the cap changed): pooled 0.717, mean facts 10.2 to 14.2. Run-to-run difference is therefore real: about one fact in four or five of a run is not reproduced by an identical second run.

OVERALL by the pre-set rule (all five must hold): the preference line is NOT safe to adopt as worded (A1, A2, A5 fail; A3 and A4 hold). Other: user share of facts S 0.43 and P 0.51 (without the session with missing author fields: 0.46 and 0.51); total facts 319 and 316; the arm-S author-field omission (19 facts in one session) did not occur in arm P.
What the data suggest instead (not part of the verdict): the gain is small and spread, not concentrated: per-session changes in user-authored facts were +3, +3, +2, +2, +2, +1, +1, +1, +1, +1, 0 (five times), -1 (three times), -3 once, plus +11 in the session whose arm S lacked author fields (an artefact). A post-hoc noise reference (not in the committed scorer): two identical-prompt runs of the 9 replicate sessions with author fields differ by 0.56 user-authored facts on average (absolute; signed +0.33), while arm P exceeds the earlier run by +1.1 on those sessions, so the sentence has a real but weak effect, about twice the run-to-run noise and about half of the bar. A one-sentence instruction at that position is too weak to move the user's stated wishes into the first 12 to 25 facts; a stronger form (for example a per-message rule: "for every user message that states a want, interest, plan or list of things they like, record one fact for it") or a separate user-preference pass would need its own pilot. Quote accuracy fell under arm P (0.908 against 0.944): the new user facts are more often not verbatim in the cited round.

### 8.3 Cost and time (the reported cost difference is a cache artefact, not an arm effect)
Per call, arm S against arm P: seconds 23.2 and 22.9; output tokens 2,987 and 2,985; uncached input 2.0 and 2.0; total input (uncached + cache creation + cache read) 16,151 and 16,184 (+0.2 percent). The reported notional cost differs (0.0652 and 0.0908, +39 percent) only because the CLI splits the same input differently: arm S read 7,496 tokens from the cache and created 8,653 per call, arm P read 975 and created 15,207 (the pricing of cache creation is higher than that of cache reads). By position inside the pair the pattern is the same (arm P read 975 whether it ran first or second; arm S read about 7,500 either way), so it follows the prompt's cache state (the cap-30 prompt had run before; the new prompt's prefix had not), not the arm's content. On tokens and time the arms are equal; do not use the 0.0908 to price arm P.

### 8.4 What this does and does not show
Shows: identical-prompt single-pass extraction is not stable at the pre-set 85 percent on these 10 sessions (0.776; one session 0.51); the one-sentence preference line raises user-authored facts weakly (+1.1 per session, about twice the noise), does not recover the wish-list items, and lowers the quote accuracy; it does not displace facts beyond ordinary run-to-run variation, does not change cost or time, and does not damage speaker attribution; the model can omit a field (the author) for a whole session. Does not show: a pool rate (20 sessions, 10 pairs; the replicate sessions are long and mostly gold); what a second pass would add (not run); whether a stronger preference instruction works; effects on retrieval or answers.

### 8.5 Recommendation for the V2 draft's part X (a recommendation only; not applied)
1. Prompt: keep `extract-v2-cap30` as the part X pilot prompt; do NOT adopt `extract-v3-pref` as worded. If preferences matter for the target questions, pilot a stronger or separate preference instruction under a new version string against the same criteria A1 to A5 (and add a quote-accuracy guard, because the quote check fell).
2. Stability: the pre-set gate failed (0.776). Do not assume a single pass is enough. Register part X's extraction as TWO independent passes per session with the union of facts (de-duplicated by quote match) as the default for the X-a pilot, and gate the second pass on its measured marginal gain (the share of gold sessions found by the union but not by one pass on the gate set), since a second pass doubles the call count (6,100 to 12,200 and 22,500 to 45,000 calls: the user's decision, with the cache-adjusted cost). Keep X-G5 as written (at least 85 percent between identical-prompt runs on at least 20 gate-set sessions) as the stability reference, now with the expectation that it fails for a single pass; the arbiter for part X is HIT@300 of the gold session (X-G4), not stability.
3. Schema: the author field can be missing (one session in this stage); the part X parser should set `author` mechanically from the speaker of the cited quote (the vendored checker already finds it) and keep the model's own author only as a cross-check, so a missing field costs nothing and a mismatch is flagged.
4. Quote accuracy: the target of at least 0.95 verbatim quotes was met at Stage X0 (0.960) and X0B (0.956) and missed here (arm S 0.944, arm P 0.908); the X-G3 gate should require the quote check at the gate-set size and repair quotes mechanically (search the cited round for the best-matching span) rather than rely on the model.
5. Pricing: use token counts, not the reported dollar cost, when comparing prompt variants (the cache state moves the dollar figure by about 40 percent at equal tokens).

## 9. POST-HOC EXPLORATORY analysis of the outputs already on disk (rules written before anything below was computed; no model call; nothing here is a pre-registered result)

Code: `emlab.xp_x0c_posthoc`. Quote match is that of Stage X0B (normalised quotes equal, or one contains the other with the shorter at least 8 characters).
(1) What a second pass buys. For the 10 replicate sessions: draw 1 = the Stage X0B output, draw 2 = the arm-S output (both `extract-v2-cap30`, identical prompt), draw 3 = the arm-P output (a prompt with one extra sentence, so a related but not identical draw). Reported: facts in draw 1, draw 2, their union de-duplicated greedily by quote match, the facts the second draw adds, how many union facts pass the quote check (a); the union curve for 1, 2 and 3 draws as the mean number of new facts each draw adds over all 6 orderings of the three draws and the 10 sessions (does the set converge or keep growing); and for the gold sessions among the 10, a reading file listing the facts present in only one of the two identical-prompt runs beside the question and answer, which the eval owner reads and records per session as "a fact needed for the question is in only one run: yes or no" (reading, not the string heuristic).
(2) Are unstable facts the unimportant ones. Facts present in only one of the two identical-prompt runs against facts present in both (pooled over the 10 sessions): the quote check pass rate, the model's author (user, assistant, missing) and the position of the cited round in thirds of the session. Reading: instability concentrated in assistant-stated detail would show as a high assistant share among the one-run facts against the both-run facts; evenly spread would show similar shares.
(3) Mechanical author and quote repair over ALL extraction outputs on disk (Stage X0 200 outputs, X0B 10, X0C 20 + 20): for every fact whose quote is verbatim in the cited round, the author set from the speaker of the turn containing the quote (user or assistant; verbatim in both speakers' turns or across a boundary is left undefined), compared with the model's author field (agreement, mismatches, missing field); up to 20 mismatches (seeded random draw) are read by the eval owner to say which is right. For quotes not verbatim in the cited round, mutually exclusive classes in this order: `other_round` (verbatim, normalised, in another round of the same session: repairable by re-pointing), `punct_cited` (equal to the cited round after removing punctuation, or every ellipsis-separated part found in order there: repairable by normalisation), `punct_other` (the same in another round), `paraphrase` (best token coverage of at least 0.8 in some round: needs a re-quote, not a mechanical repair), and `ungrounded` (none of these). "Truly ungrounded" = paraphrase plus ungrounded.
